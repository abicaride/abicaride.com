## Central case hypothesis

Abi's work at imaginArt is potentially stronger as one broad professional case
than as a collection of disconnected deliverables.

The recurring challenge was turning specialist audiovisual information and
business needs into clear, useful content that a professional audience could
understand and act on across different channels.

> **Making complex B2B communication clearer and more useful**

web, email and events. It should not frame Abi as someone who simply “did a bit
of everything”. Breadth is evidence of her ability to apply the same core skill
in different contexts, not a substitute for a clear professional position.

## Example 1 — Turtle AV

### Why it belongs

Turtle is the clearest available example of content design applied to a
technical product. Abi confirms that she structured the page herself. No formal
brief survives.

### Content problem

Technical information needed to become a usable B2B web experience rather than
a flat specification list.

Working conceptual sequence:

```text
Value proposition
→ Product introduction
→ Key characteristics / benefits
→ Product solutions
→ Technical detail
→ Applications / use cases
→ Contact CTA
```

This sequence is an analytical model for the case study. It should be checked
against the public work before final publication and should not be presented as
a recovered formal brief.

### Portfolio angle

> **From technical information to usable product content**

The relevant skill is not personally knowing every AV specification. It is
working with specialists, preserving technical truth and structuring the
information for a professional user.

### Original visual idea

Create an original content-transformation diagram:

```text
RAW TECHNICAL INPUT
4K / Dante / AES67 / PoE / HDR / latency / redundancy / compatibility

↓

CONTENT QUESTIONS
What is it?
Why does it matter?
Which solution fits?
Where can it be used?
What should the reader do next?

↓

STRUCTURED PRODUCT PAGE
```

This must be an original portfolio visual, not a copy of imaginArt's page.

## Example 2 — Mundo BrightSign

### Why it belongs

Mundo BrightSign is a strong example of content optimization with an observed
outcome.

### Earlier performance

Approximate open rate: **~24%**.

### What changed

According to Abi:

- the general audience remained the same;
- the product/content context was comparable;
- the tone became closer without losing professionalism;
- an emoji was placed at the beginning of the subject;
- the CTA moved into the first viewport / above the fold;
- the editorial framing was revised.

### Observed result

Approximate open rate: **~34%**.

Internal representation:

```text
~24% → ~34%
+10 percentage points approx.
```

### Accuracy rule

This was **not an A/B test**.

Good direction for future copy:

> “After the revised editorial approach, open rate increased from roughly 24%
> to 34%.”

Avoid:

> “The new subject line increased opens by X%.”

Do not claim causal proof for the subject, emoji, tone, CTA placement or any
single change. The result followed a combined editorial revision, and the
remembered figures are approximate.

### Original visual idea

```text
EARLIER APPROACH          REVISED APPROACH

Subject                   Emoji + revised subject
Intro                     Closer professional tone
Content                   CTA above the fold
Content                   Content
CTA                       Additional content

~24%                      ~34%
```

The visual should abstract information hierarchy. It must not recreate the
company newsletter as portfolio artwork.
